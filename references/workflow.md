# Research workflow

1. Map official sources before general discovery. Locate the gazette, consolidated statutes, legislature and committees, regulator and ministry portals, case law repositories, consultations and archived versions. Verify authority by legal mandate or an official cross-link, publisher provenance, available date range and access conditions. Use institutional and reference sources where appropriate, for example public universities, official statistics, civil society or professional associations. Record missing source classes.
2. Seed searches with the question, jurisdiction (including subnational competence), verified legal terminology, date range and exact citations. Run terms and equivalents in the selected search languages. Keep searches, result counts where offered, URLs and actual timestamps. Record searches as executed only after running them.
3. Trace citations backwards and forwards, including legislative proposals, committee revisions, adopted instruments, effective dates, implementation evidence, opposition and public consultation submissions. Preserve evidence of attribution and genuinely conflicting material. Never treat an overview as a substitute for a primary instrument.
4. Maintain jurisdictional observations and resumable search tasks with `research_state.py`. A source observation records ownership and function evidence and access capabilities. Use `record --input EVENTS.json`, `sources --country CODE`, `history` and `resume`. Log blocked, failed and unexecuted work distinctly. Use public endpoints and respect service terms, reasonable rates, archives and licenses. External browsing and acquisition are performed by the executing agent using tools available in its environment.
5. Assess the applicable coverage dimensions and substantive gaps. Default review areas include law and historical amendments, preparatory documents, legislative proceedings, international sources, advocacy, implementation and statistics, scholarly research, and case law. These are audit dimensions rather than default filing categories. Use topic-specific coverage requirements where appropriate. Stop broad searches when verified source classes and key citations have been checked, new results are mainly duplicates or peripheral, and remaining gaps are recorded. Escalate significant evidence gaps for targeted research. Switch to Full-text Retrieval when the higher-value work is recovering existing material. Never use a fixed document count as evidence of completeness.
